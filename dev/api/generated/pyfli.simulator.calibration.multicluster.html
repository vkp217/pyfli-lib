Depth of the density valley between two components: the pixels assigned to either are projected onto the line joining the means, a Gaussian KDE (Scott bandwidth) of the projection is evaluated between the two projected means, and the minimum is divided by the smaller of the densities at the means.

detect_clusters(decay, mask, max_components=4, separation=2.0, min_fraction=0.05, use_intensity=False, n_fit=20000, smooth=True, seed=0, max_dip_ratio=0.5)[source]#

Find well-separated pixel populations inside mask; see the module docstring.

Two mixture components stay separate only if Ashman’s D is at least separation and the pixel density projected onto the line joining their means has a real dip between them (_dip_ratio() below max_dip_ratio). A mixture fitted to a few hundred pixels of one skewed population can reach D > 2 by chance, but its projected density has no dip (ratio ~0.7-1), while separate populations give ~0.

Per-pixel IRF cube (H, W, T) with the calibrated OffsetGen behaviour: each pixel gets roll(template, round(a)) + b, a ~ U(a_range), b ~ U(b_range). It is scaled to peak because pyfli’s irf_picker only accepts 3D IRFs whose peak exceeds 500 (each pixel is sum-normalised by the generator anyway).
